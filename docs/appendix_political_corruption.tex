\subsection{Purpose and Procedure}

This exploratory analysis describes recurring themes in the
political-corruption corpus. It was not used to code the article-level outcome
variables, assess their validity, or estimate inferential models. The resulting
topics should therefore be read as patterns of coverage rather than as an
exhaustive or validated taxonomy of corruption types.

The analysis began with a country--year-stratified sample of
\DescriptiveTopicSourceSampleN{} articles, drawing at most 50 articles from each
non-empty stratum and retaining inverse sampling-probability weights. A
preliminary sensitivity analysis clustered the original multilingual articles,
but manual inspection showed substantial clustering by publication language,
country, and recurring cases. That specification was archived rather than used
for substantive interpretation.

For the retained specification, GPT-5.1 transformed every sampled article into
a short, standardized English description of the alleged corrupt practice,
institutional setting, and central response. The prompt removed names,
countries, outlets, dates, exact amounts, and other case identifiers. It marked
\DescriptiveTopicBoundaryN{} articles without a sufficiently clear
political-corruption focus as boundary or unclear; these records remain in the
audit data but were not clustered. These standardized article-level
descriptions are referred to below as abstractions.

BERTopic embedded the \DescriptiveTopicUsableN{} usable abstractions with
\texttt{intfloat/}\allowbreak\texttt{multilingual-e5-large}, reduced their
dimensionality with UMAP, formed density-based clusters with HDBSCAN, and
represented the resulting topics with class-based TF--IDF. The number of topics
was not fixed in advance. A documented grid varied UMAP neighborhood size,
minimum cluster size, density requirement, and HDBSCAN extraction method. Each
candidate was refitted on five 80\% resamples. Eligibility required four to
twelve topics, at least 55\% assignment of usable abstractions, no topic larger
than 40\% of assigned cases, country--topic normalized mutual information no
higher than .25, and at least 35\% mean overlap in non-outlier assignments.
Selection then prioritized mean resample adjusted Rand agreement, followed by
assignment overlap, cluster persistence, and relative validity.

GPT-5.1 proposed labels and descriptions from representative abstractions. The
topic labels were subsequently reviewed and standardized for neutral academic
wording without changing any topic assignments. Prompts, examples, raw model
responses, candidate diagnostics, and checksummed manifests were retained.

\subsection{Descriptive Results}

The selected specification produced \DescriptiveTopicN{} topics and assigned
\DescriptiveTopicInlierN{} of the \DescriptiveTopicUsableN{} usable
abstractions. HDBSCAN treated \DescriptiveTopicOutlierN{}
(\DescriptiveTopicOutlierShare\%) as outliers. The selected solution achieved a
mean resample adjusted Rand index of \DescriptiveTopicMeanResampleARI{} and a
minimum resample value of \DescriptiveTopicMinResampleARI{}. Country--topic
normalized mutual information was \DescriptiveTopicCountryNMI{}, and no topic
was dominated by a single publication country.

Table~\ref{tab:pc-descriptive-topics} reports the weighted distribution of the
direct descriptive topics among assigned abstractions. The largest theme was
procurement and public-revenue corruption, followed by prosecution of senior
public officials, electoral corruption and incumbent abuse, anti-corruption
reform and rule-of-law oversight, and campaign and party finance. These themes
mix alleged practices with institutional responses and consequently do not map
one-to-one onto the article-level individualized/systemic framing variable.
