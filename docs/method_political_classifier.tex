\subsection{Data Collection and Political-Corruption Classifier}
\label{sec:method-political-classifier}

News articles were collected as part of the RESPOND multilingual news corpus. The data-collection workflow queried the News API using country-specific corruption keyword dictionaries with declensions and morphological variants. The collection code and keyword-generation notebooks are maintained in the related RESPOND media repository.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/annekroon/RESPOND_media/tree/main/data-collection/news-collection/news-api}} The present repository starts from the exported country-level article files and implements the cleaning, deduplication, classification, and validation workflow used to identify articles primarily about political corruption.

The first stage cleaned and deduplicated the country-level news files. Article text was standardized by using the combined title/body field available in the exported data and storing it as \texttt{article\_text}. Text normalization removed excess whitespace and repaired common character-encoding artefacts. Rows marked as duplicates by the source data were removed, articles with missing or very short text were excluded, and exact and near-exact duplicates were removed using normalized text hashes. The resulting cleaned corpus contained 2,264,916 articles from Bulgaria, France, Hungary, Italy, the Netherlands, Serbia, Sweden, Ukraine, and the United Kingdom.

The cleaned corpus was subsequently restricted to conventional journalistic outlets. Each country--source pair was evaluated on whether the outlet was genuinely based in the assigned country, produced original reporting or exercised identifiable editorial curation, and did not function primarily as an official repository, press-release distributor, or automated aggregator. Only sources coded \texttt{conventional\_journalism = Yes} in the source-inclusion workbook were retained; sources coded No, left unresolved, or missing from the workbook were excluded. This screen reduced the cleaned corruption-query corpus to 1,958,721 articles. Source eligibility was applied before constructing the silver-labelled training sample and before scoring the final corpus.

The second stage trained a classifier to distinguish articles primarily about political corruption from articles that mention corruption only incidentally or concern non-political misconduct. Political corruption was defined as the misuse of public or political office, authority, or resources for private or political gain. The positive class includes articles focused on allegations, investigations, charges, or proven instances of corruption by political officials. Articles focused on private fraud, ordinary crime, or misconduct by non-political actors were treated as negative cases.

Because manual labels were limited, we used a silver-labeling strategy. A country-balanced candidate set was sampled from the source-filtered corpus and sent to the UvA LLM proxy for structured political-corruption labels. These labels were treated as silver labels for model training rather than as final human annotations. A manually annotated validation set was kept separate for evaluation. After an initial country-level validation check showed low positive support for the United Kingdom, a smaller set of UK cases was manually reviewed and added to the validation benchmark as a validation-only supplement. The fixed human benchmark was retained intact rather than being used for model training.

Candidate classifiers were evaluated against the human validation benchmark. The final selected classifier used \texttt{intfloat/multilingual-e5-large} sentence embeddings and a class-balanced logistic regression model trained on the full source-filtered silver-labelled training set. This set contained 4,497 LLM-labelled articles after excluding unusable cases and duplicate article identifiers. The decision threshold was set to 0.50 based on validation-set threshold sweeps. The final validation set contained 502 manually labelled articles, including 141 political-corruption articles. On this validation set, the final classifier achieved accuracy = 0.829, political-corruption precision = 0.654, political-corruption recall = 0.830, political-corruption F1 = 0.731, macro F1 = 0.803, and weighted F1 = 0.834. The main classifier validation results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:pc-classifier-comparison-main}; the threshold sweep and country-level validation results are provided in Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:pc-classifier-threshold-sweep} and \ref{tab:pc-classifier-country-validation}.

The final classifier was applied to the source-filtered country files. Articles with predicted probabilities at or above the 0.50 threshold were retained, producing a final political-corruption corpus of 459,674 articles, or 23.47\% of the source-filtered corruption-query corpus. These articles form the input for subsequent analyses of victim visibility, corruption framing, case location, accused-actor visibility, and attention over time. For descriptive attention analyses, the numerator is the number of final political-corruption articles in a country-period. The denominator comes from a separate NewsAPI country-period total-news count series rather than from the corruption-query corpus. Because the available total-news counts are not source-specific, this denominator represents the broader collected news universe and is not filtered using the outlet-inclusion workbook.
